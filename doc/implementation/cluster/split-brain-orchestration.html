Split-brain orchestration across three datacenters

Three clouds, one per DC. On a split, DC1 is cut off from its peer and the arbitrator, so it becomes the MINORITY — its master steps down. DC2 + DC3 keep the MAJORITY and promote a slave to NEW MASTER. What differs is the recovery — so we start from the Free plan to see where we come from, then the Support plan that removes the pain, then the symmetric split where repman must fence both sides.

Free Plan Orchestration

No dedicated arbitrator — a third repman stands in for DC3.

The catch: tie-breaking is weaker, so the split lingers. Replication is cut, so the isolated OLD MASTER keeps writing binlogs no slave consumes — a growing pile of delta transactions that can't be flashed back. The only way home is a full RESTORE FROM BACKUP — slow and heavy. The Support plan avoids it entirely: the dedicated arbitrator resolves fast, and flashback / mariabackup rejoin recover the old master in place.

Support Plan Orchestration

Dedicated arbitrator in DC3 (signal18) — the strong tie-breaker.

Outcome: DC2 + DC3 are the majority — db2 is promoted to NEW MASTER. DC1 is the minority; db1 steps down to OLD MASTER, read-only. Its divergence is a single flashback-able delta — rejoin in place, seconds.

Lose–Lose Orchestration · Symmetric Split

Both DCs still reach the arbitrator — but not each other. Neither can prove a safe majority, so repman fences both masters.

Outcome: the split is symmetric — each side still reaches the arbitrator, so each could naïvely read a 2-of-3 majority and promote. To stop a real split-brain, repman does the safe thing and grants neither: both proxies are cut and both masters are FTWRL-frozen. The price is a full write outage until an operator (or a directional-failback policy) elects a survivor — but there is zero divergence, so recovery is a clean unfreeze, never a restore.
